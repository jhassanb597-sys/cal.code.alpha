📖 A Brief History of Calculators

The Beginning — The Abacus (c. 2000 BC): Long before electricity or silicon, ancient civilizations used the abacus — a simple frame with sliding beads — to perform arithmetic. It was the world's first "calculator," relied upon by merchants and scholars for thousands of years. In the 17th century, Blaise Pascal invented the Pascaline, a mechanical device that could add and subtract using geared wheels — a breakthrough that laid the foundation for modern computing.

The Electronic Revolution (1960s–1970s): The invention of the transistor and integrated circuit changed everything. In 1967, Texas Instruments created the first handheld electronic calculator. By the mid-1970s, pocket calculators became affordable and replaced slide rules in classrooms and offices worldwide. These devices could perform complex operations in milliseconds — a feat that once required entire rooms of machinery.

Today's Calculators: Modern calculators have evolved into everything from scientific graphing calculators to smartphone apps with built-in AI. They fit in your pocket, solve algebraic equations, plot graphs, and even integrate with cloud-based tools. Yet, despite all this power, the fundamental purpose remains the same: helping humans compute quickly and accurately — from the abacus to the app.
